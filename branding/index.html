01

Brand
foundation

Everything in this book exists to protect one idea: that the food is the reason people come, and nothing about how we present it should get in the way of that.

What we are

Bite More is a halal Italian-American fast-casual kitchen. Bold food, generous portions, made from scratch, priced and paced for how people actually eat.

Mission

To deliver bold, accessible, high-quality food that honors both halal integrity and the rich tradition of Italian-American cooking — at a price point and speed that fits modern life.

Vision

To become America's first nationally recognized halal Italian-American restaurant brand — proving that halal food belongs on every main street, in every market, without compromise.

The halal rule

Read this before writing anything

Halal is a sourcing standard, not our audience. We market bold Italian-American food to everyone in Atlanta. Halal is how we buy — it is never the headline, the hook, or the reason we give someone to order.

In practice: halal appears once per page or panel, as a badge or a supporting line. If a piece of marketing leads with halal, it's wrong and needs a rewrite. The test — if a non-halal-observant customer read this, would they still want the food? If not, fix it.

Positioning in one line

The Italian-American food you already love, cooked properly, that everybody at the table can eat.

02

Voice
& tone

We sound like a confident cook, not a marketing department. Short sentences. Specific nouns. No adjectives doing work a fact could do.

Four traits

TraitMeansDoesn't mean
DirectSay the thing. "Cooked to order." "Open seven days."Blunt or cold. We're warm, we're just not wordy.
Specific"Two patties smashed hard enough to get lace on the edges."Vague praise. "Delicious," "amazing," "the best" mean nothing.
GenerousBig portions, big pans, come hungry. Abundance is the feeling.Sloppy. Generous and careful aren't opposites.
UnfussyFast-casual confidence. We're not a white-tablecloth restaurant.Careless about quality. We're precise about the cooking.

Voice in practice

Write this

  • "Alfredo built in the pan, not poured from a jug."
  • "Garlic toast that shows up hot."
  • "Buckhead: 11am–12am Mon–Wed, 1am Thu, 3am Fri–Sat, 1am Sun. Duluth: 11am–9pm Mon–Wed, 11am–11pm Thu–Sun."
  • "Every protein is halal."
  • "Nobody remembers a sandwich platter."

Not this

  • "Our chefs craft each dish with passion and love."
  • "Experience a culinary journey like no other."
  • "The #1 halal restaurant in Atlanta!!"
  • "Finally, halal food you can trust."
  • "Delicious food at affordable prices."

Never

Exclamation marks in headlines. Emoji in headlines or on packaging. All-caps sentences (all-caps is for display type, not for shouting). Fake urgency. Fake scarcity. Invented customer quotes — every testimonial we publish must be traceable to a real review or a signed release.

Action required — the two oranges don't match

The packaging specifies Pantone 172 C (#FA4616). The website is currently running #FA4616. Side by side, the web one reads noticeably more yellow. A customer holding the bag while looking at the site will see two different brands.

Recommendation: keep Pantone 172 C as the master and update the site to #FA4616. Change one line in assets/styles.css and the whole site follows. Alternatively, keep the web value and re-spec the print — but pick one, and do it before the next print run.

Proportion

Roughly 60% ground, 30% black, 10% orange. Orange is an accent — when it covers large areas it stops reading as an accent and the brand loses its punch. The one exception is a full orange block used deliberately as a section break.

The one-word rule

Fraunces italic exists to emphasize a single word inside an Archivo headline. One or two words maximum. Any longer and the size difference between the two typefaces becomes obvious and the line looks broken.

It must be set at 1.2em of the surrounding display type with a slight baseline nudge, because it's lowercase sitting next to caps. Never use Fraunces for body copy, menus, prices or packaging.

Script — packaging only

HandwrittenPackaging & merch only
good food, good mood

The handwritten script carries the warmth on bags, cups and boxes. It's for taglines and short asides only — never for information a customer needs to read, never for prices, allergens, hours or addresses, and never on the website.

Specimen note

The script shown here is a stand-in. Whoever holds the packaging source file should record the actual font name and license in this book, so signage and merch vendors can match it instead of guessing. This is the single most common way a brand drifts.

Hierarchy rules

RuleWhy
One display headline per viewTwo competing headlines means neither wins.
Body copy never below 14px / 8ptMenus get read in dim rooms by people in a hurry.
Max 3 type sizes per pieceMore than three reads as a flyer, not a brand.
Never stretch or condense type manuallyArchivo has a real width axis — use it.
Line length 50–70 charactersLonger and people stop reading mid-paragraph.

06

Illustration
& badges

Loose, hand-drawn, single-weight line art in orange or black. The doodles are what make the packaging feel like a neighborhood shop instead of a chain.

Illustration rules

Do

  • Single line weight, hand-drawn feel, slightly imperfect
  • Spot color only — orange or black, never both in one drawing
  • Subjects we actually sell: burger, pasta bowl, wings, pizza slice, fries
  • Supporting marks: hearts, sparkles, basil leaves, steam lines
  • Scattered with breathing room, never crowded edge to edge

Don't

  • Photorealistic or 3D rendered food
  • Gradients, shading or fills inside the line art
  • Stock clip art that doesn't match the line weight
  • Drawing dishes we don't serve
  • Mixing illustration styles on one piece

The badge trio

These three always appear together, in this order, at equal size. They're a set — never use one alone, never reorder them, never add a fourth.

◗
100% Halal

Sourcing standard. Stated plainly, never as a headline.

❧
Fresh Ingredients

Scratch sauces, in-house dough, prepped daily.

▤
Made to Order

Nothing sits in a warmer waiting for you.

Icons shown are placeholders — use the line-art icons from the packaging source file.

Why the trio works

Halal sits between two food-quality claims rather than standing alone. That's deliberate and it's the correct treatment everywhere: it reads as one of three quality standards, not as the pitch. Keep this pattern on menus, signage, delivery-app storefronts and the website.

Packaging

The bag is our most-seen piece of brand. It travels through offices, dorms and living rooms where we have no other presence.

Kraft warning

Pantone 172 C on natural kraft prints duller and browner than it looks on screen. If the orange loses its punch on the proof, the fix is a white flood behind the orange areas, not a brighter ink. Approve on paper, never on a monitor.

Extending the system

Cups

Vertical wordmark, one tagline, illustration band at the base. No badge trio — not enough room.

Pizza / entrée boxes

Horizontal wordmark centered on the lid, pattern on the sides, badge trio on one short edge.

Napkins

Wordmark or a single heart in orange. One element only.

Stickers & seals

Circular badge lockup, orange on cream. Used to seal bags — doubles as tamper-evidence for delivery.

Staff apparel

Wordmark on the chest in one color. Black tee with bone wordmark, or cream with black.

Catering trays

Printed label band with wordmark, dish name and the badge trio. Every tray gets labeled.

Page furniture

Buttons

Orange fill with black text, 2px radius, 0.16em tracked uppercase. Secondary is a bordered ghost button.

Orange ticker

The scrolling orange strip separates the hero from the page. One per page, never two.

Orange block

One full-orange section per page as a break — usually the franchise or catering call to action.

Menu rows

Photo, name, description, tag. Rows, not cards — a menu should read like a menu.

Badge trio

Carry it from the packaging onto the site and delivery-app storefronts. Same order, same weight.

Motion

Subtle only: fade-and-rise on scroll, the ticker, hover states. Everything respects reduced-motion.

Third-party storefronts count

DoorDash, UberEats and Grubhub are brand surfaces. Same header image, same item names as the menu here, same descriptions, badge trio in the store description. Most first impressions happen there, not on our own site.

Photography
& video

Warm, close, a little messy. Food that looks like food, not like a rendering.

Do

  • Warm light, slightly overhead or 45°, shallow depth
  • Close enough to see texture — lace on the patty, pull on the cheese
  • Real portions in real packaging on real surfaces
  • Hands in frame: passing, pulling, dipping
  • Steam, sauce on the rim, a little imperfection

Don't

  • Cold blue or green-tinted light
  • Heavy filters, HDR, or oversaturated orange food
  • Styled portions smaller than what we actually serve
  • Stock photography of food we don't make
  • Cluttered backgrounds that fight the dish

Video

UseSpecRule
Website hero loop1920×1080, 12–20s, under 6 MBNo on-screen text. Seamless loop. Calm zone where the headline sits.
Testimonials1920×1080, 45–90sBurned-in subtitles, always. Real customers, one specific point each.

Never fake a testimonial

Every quote we publish must come from a real review or a customer who signed a release. Invented testimonials are a legal exposure and they're the fastest way to lose a room full of regulars who know better.

Before you
publish

Run anything — a post, a menu, a bag proof, a page — through this before it ships.

  • Is the wordmark the supplied file, at the right color split, with clear space respected?
  • Is the orange Pantone 172 C / #FA4616 — the same value as everything else we've printed?
  • Is orange under about 10% of the surface, used as an accent rather than a field?
  • Is all display type Archivo at weight 900, width 118 — never stretched by hand?
  • If there's a Fraunces italic accent, is it one or two words at 1.2em?
  • Is the script used for a tagline only, and nowhere a customer needs to read information?
  • Does halal appear once, as a badge or a supporting line — never as the headline?
  • Would a customer who doesn't eat halal still want this food after reading it?
  • Is every claim, quote and review real and traceable?
  • Are hours, phone numbers, addresses and the URL correct and current?
  • Does every dish named actually exist on the menu at both locations today?
  • For print: has a physical proof been approved on the actual substrate?
